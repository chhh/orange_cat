The uncomfortable pattern: the cat has not got in on a single night since 09-07 — including three nights when the water was completely non-functional, and one when nothing fired at all.

That cuts against the water being what is keeping it out. Either the sound alone is sufficient, or the cannon's visible presence is doing the work, or the cat's motivation has simply dropped for reasons that have nothing to do with us. Six nights of the same outcome under four different system states is a streak with very little explanatory power — and the honest reading is that we still cannot say which of our measures, if any, is responsible.

What was fixed yesterday

Dave went to Dima's and left the laptop there for a fortnight. Three things changed, all of them for the better:

  • The tunnel is gone from the path. Disabled and un-enabled. It had been actively harmful on-site: our config routes 192.168.1.133/32 into the tunnel, and Home Assistant is 192.168.1.133 on the LAN we now sit on — so every time wg0 came up it hijacked the route to HA. That was the afternoon's flap storm, and it was ours, not Dima's.
  • The Zigbee integration was reloaded, restoring the valve after two dead nights. The coordinator had never come back from HA's 09-10 restart.
  • Water was confirmed to reach the patio — the first time anyone has seen it. Every fire logged since 09-07 had simply been assumed to work.

Two things the test fire taught us

The line purges slowly. A 3-second burst produced nothing visible; an 8-second burst worked. Whether the deterrent's real pattern — three pulses of a few tenths of a second — delivers water once the line is primed is still unknown. Testing stopped before that was settled, and it remains the most important open question about the water.

A short pulse can strand the valve open. A 0.45s test pulse timed out on its close and left the valve open about 15 seconds — the same FINAL CLOSE FAILED signature as the 05:34 engagement the morning before, and the exact pattern the deterrent fires three times per engagement.

But the third cutoff is now proven rather than assumed. Dave checked Home Assistant's own Traces: automation.cat_sprayer_failsafe_off fired at precisely that moment — while our direct API turn_off was timing out. So the failsafe runs on a more reliable path than the commands we issue, and survives conditions where our path is already failing. Combined with the fact that every route to opening the valve goes through HA — so a dead HA cannot open it — the flood risk is acceptable. The residual is narrow: HA dying within ten seconds of an open, before the failsafe runs.

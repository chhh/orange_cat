The sequence now has a shape worth naming: one aversive event, then silence. The stray was driven off the patio by the spray on 09-07/08 and has not been seen since — two consecutive nights, with the food still out and nothing else changed.

That is what avoidance learning looks like. It is also what a cat ranging somewhere else for two nights looks like, and nothing in the record distinguishes them. Two nights is not a result; it is the beginning of one. What would make it a result is the same absence continuing for another three or four nights, or the cat returning, meeting the spray again, and leaving faster than last time.

One consequence of the startle mechanism is worth holding onto here. If the spray works by the crack of the valve rather than by wetting — and last night's correction established that it does — then the habituation risk lives entirely in engagements. A cat that stays away cannot habituate. It also cannot be re-taught. So a long absence is good news that quietly erodes: the lesson fades with time, and the first return visit is the one that tells us whether it held.

The deterrent was inert for four and a half minutes

Home Assistant went down twice, 14 minutes apart. The first was a clean service stop that HA logged itself; the second took the whole host off the network. Both self-resolved. The shape — restart the service, then reboot the machine — reads like an update on Dima's box rather than a failing one.

The important part is what "HA is down" actually means for us. It is not "the water is unavailable". Both speakers are HA media_player calls and the valve is Zigbee behind HA, so sound dies with the water. For those 4.4 minutes there was no action available at all — no siren, no drill, no spray.

Detection was never touched, and nothing on our own box wobbled either: the 06:50 scorecard reports AC mains up, zero ACPI hogs, the watchdog armed at 60, detector and tunnel active, patrol alive, and 10 motion POSTs delivered across the window — the last at 05:44:57, matching the final tuxedo sighting to the second. The cameras and the patrol reach the NVR through the tunnel and never speak to HA, so the stream stayed hot with zero reconnects the whole time. That asymmetry is the finding: everything we use to see lives on our side; everything we use to act lives on Dima's LAN, outside our control, with no fallback path.

All three readiness probes answer wrong

The outages happened to expose this cleanly, because the two failures had different shapes. Asking "is the deterrent ready?" three plausible ways gives three different wrong answers:

ping 192.168.1.133
what the health watch does
✗
Caught the second outage, because the host was genuinely gone. Would have sailed straight through the first — the machine answers ping perfectly well while Home Assistant is stopped.
GET /api/
the obvious fix
✗
Catches both outages, then lies during recovery. At 00:40:02 the API returned 200 while the valve, the failsafe automation and both speakers were still missing. Registration lagged the API by about 90 seconds.
GET /api/states/
switch.cat_sprayer
the honest probe
✓
The valve entity existing is the only signal that means the deterrent can actually fire. This is what confirmed recovery at 00:40:15.

So "Home Assistant is up" overstates readiness by a minute and a half, and "the host pings" can overstate it indefinitely. A future session reading a green health check during an HA restart would have no idea the deterrent was dead.

Fixes queued for daylight

  • Health watch, line 36: replace the ping 192.168.1.133 check with a query for switch.cat_sprayer. Nothing was changed live — editing a safety watch inside the deterrent window is not worth the coverage gap for a fix that can wait for morning.
  • No fallback actuator path is a real single point of failure, now demonstrated rather than theorised. Worth scoping what, if anything, could act while HA is down — Dima's talk.py Pi is the only other speaker route and it is powered off.
  • Answered, 09-10: Dima confirms he updated and rebooted Home Assistant last night. The cause is benign — but that makes this recurring, not a one-off. Every future HA update will disable the deterrent for several minutes, silently, and the next one may not land on an empty night. Worth asking Dima to keep updates outside 22:00–06:00: a one-line social fix that removes the whole class more cheaply than any code.

The Cached Valve

The stray came back, the siren turned it around, and it never reached the door. The water was also supposed to fire — and could not, because it had been dead for two nights while every instrument reported it healthy.

Night of 2026-09-11 → 12 · window 21:45–06:00 · blind 21:45–01:29 · one engagement, no entry

05:34:36 — first sighting in eight nights that our own cameras caught. 34.2% ginger at the gate end. Four seconds later the siren fired, then the drill; the cat receded and was gone. No entry, no meal.

So the cat was holding its distance at the gate end, not retreating, when the siren went off — and then receded fast. That is a cleaner reversal than any previous sound engagement has produced, and the 4-second trigger-to-decision is the quickest yet.

But do not book it as a win. Yesterday's report recorded the opposite condition: on 09-11 at 05:20 the cat came, stopped, and left with nothing whatsoever firing. Same outcome, no deterrent. One clean-looking reversal does not undo that, and the honest position is still that we cannot attribute departures to our actions yet.

What tonight adds is a sharper version of the question. The height trace is the first time we have watched the cat stop closing and start receding within two seconds of a sound. If that pattern repeats, it is hard to explain as coincidence. If the next engagement looks like 09-11 instead, it was.

The water was dead, and had been for two nights

The fire commanded water and got timed out, twice — once on the open, once on the safety close. Chasing that produced the real finding of the night.

36 of Home Assistant's 440 entities are frozen at exactly 2026-09-10T07:40:2x — the instant HA finished restarting two nights ago. Several are explicitly flagged restored: true. The frozen set is the Zigbee devices: switch.cat_sprayer, its battery sensor, its identify button, plus the SONOFF plug and three dimmers. Meanwhile 28 other entities updated in the last half hour, so HA itself is perfectly healthy.

The Zigbee integration never came back. Which means:

The water deterrent was non-functional for 09-10/11 and 09-11/12

Two full nights. Sound was unaffected — the speakers are media_player, a different integration — so the deterrent was running at half strength and reporting full readiness.

There was no flood

With no integration loaded, HA had no path to deliver turn_on. The valve never actuated; it holds the off position it had on 09-10. The "valve may be OPEN" line is deter.py being properly paranoid.

The probe that lied — and I was the one who recommended it

Yesterday's report argued that ping and GET /api/ both overstate readiness, and that the honest question to ask is whether the valve entity exists. That advice was wrong, and this is the fourth instrument in five nights to report health while the thing behind it was broken:

ping 192.168.1.133
Sails through an HA service stop — the host answers while HA is dead.
GET /api/
Returns 200 while entities are still registering. Overstated readiness by ~90 seconds on 09-10.
systemctl is-active
wg-quick@wg0
Read active through a 22-hour dead tunnel. The overnight report printed "tunnel: active" the whole time.
GET /api/states/
switch.cat_sprayer
Returned off and battery 100% for two days while the device was unreachable. My "ready for tonight" check at 20:02 on 09-10 and my post-recovery check at 01:30 tonight were both reading restored cache. The flood watch polls this same entity every three seconds and never noticed.

What actually detected it was trying to use it. The only honest test of an actuator is actuation — and we cannot fire the valve to test it without warning Dima first, which is the right rule and not one to bend.

There is, however, a checkable proxy that would have caught this. A restored entity gives itself away two ways: the restored: true attribute, and a last_updated that has not moved since the last HA restart. Readiness is not "the entity exists" — it is "the entity exists and its reading is fresh." The flood watch already fetches this JSON every three seconds; it has the timestamp in hand and throws it away.

Coverage, and what we cannot say

The tunnel outage from the previous night ran until 01:29:26 — 21 hours 58 minutes in total. So tonight splits: blind from 21:45 to 01:29, watched from 01:29 to 06:00. The blind stretch covers the 23h, 00h and 01h bands, which hold 7 of the 50 archived sightings; the watched stretch covers 02h–05h, which hold 18. The engagement we caught was at 05:34, near the end of the covered half.

Box health on our side was clean throughout: mains power, zero ACPI hogs, watchdog armed, patrol alive, 19 unbroken heartbeats, stream hot from 01:29 with zero reconnects.

Fixes

  • Dima: reload the Zigbee integration in Home Assistant (Settings → Devices & Services), or restart HA, or re-seat the coordinator. Nothing at our end can restore the valve. Until then the deterrent is sound-only.
  • Add a freshness test to the flood watch. Require last_updated to be recent and restored to be absent before treating the valve as ready. This is the one change that would have caught tonight's fault two nights earlier.
  • Stop printing "tunnel: active" in the overnight report — it is a systemd unit's start result, not a live link. A ping to 192.168.7.1 would be honest.
  • Still open from before: HA updates should avoid 22:00–06:00, since a restart is what triggered this whole chain.

Questions

  1. Does the height reversal repeat? That is the discriminating observation between "the sound works" and "the cat leaves anyway", and tonight gave us the first clean instance.
  2. How many other readiness checks are reading cache? Four have now lied in five nights. The pattern is not bad luck; it is that we keep asking whether a component is present rather than whether it works.
  3. Did anything come before 01:29? Unknowable from here. Dima's NVR would still have it.

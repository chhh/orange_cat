Both errors come from string matching. overnight-report.sh counts grep -c "ORANGE CAT", and this sighting arrived through the low-confidence path as ~orange-ish. It counts grep -c "deterrent decision: fired", and this engagement ended as decision: failed — because the sound failed, even though the water had already gone out.

The deeper problem is the word "failed". In deter.py a playback failure returns "failed" for the whole decision, regardless of what the water did. Reaction capture is gated on decision == "fired", so nothing was recorded. The ring buffer in /dev/shm holds about twelve seconds, so the footage was gone within moments.

That is unlucky beyond the bookkeeping: this was the first time the real pulse pattern has fired at a live cat since the on-site test revealed that the line purges slowly — a three-second burst produced nothing visible, an eight-second burst worked. Whether 0.15-second pulses put water on the patio is the single most important unknown about the hardware, this engagement was the test, and we have no footage of it.

Recoverable

Dima's NVR records continuously and independently. The 02:14 clip should still exist on his box, and it would answer the pulse question outright. Worth asking for.

Not recoverable

Our own footage. Twelve seconds of ring buffer, no capture triggered. Nothing on this machine survives from 02:14.

The sound failure

HTTP Error 500 from Home Assistant's media player. Notably not the cause that had been predicted for this fortnight: the DHCP lease was unchanged at 192.168.1.14, both sound URLs returned 200 when checked seconds later, and both speakers read idle and reachable. The cause is unknown.

It also cannot be investigated the obvious way. Testing means playing audio through a speaker at Dima's house, and the standing rule is that nothing plays without him being warned first — which is the correct rule and not one to bend at two in the morning for a diagnostic. So this stays open until it either recurs or can be tested by daylight with his agreement.

Worth keeping in proportion: the water half worked. The valve was commanded, actuated and closed cleanly, with no timeout and no stuck-open event. On current evidence the water is the more reliable half of the deterrent as well as the more plausible one.

Fixes this night argues for

  • Capture on water, not only on sound. Gate reaction capture on the water having fired as well as decision == "fired". This night's footage was lost to a one-word condition.

  • Rename the outcome. "failed" for a night that delivered three pulses of water is actively misleading to whoever reads the log next.

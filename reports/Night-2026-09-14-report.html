What fits this, and what doesn't

A roughly 90% drop in appearances, beginning the night the water was first armed. The obvious reading is that the water is repelling the cat nightly. That reading does not survive the detail.

The water was completely non-functional for the nights of 09-09/10, 09-10/11 and 09-11/12 — Home Assistant's Zigbee integration never came back from a restart, and the valve was an unreachable cache entry the whole time. On 09-10/11 nothing fired at all, and the cat came, stopped short and left anyway. Appearances stayed at the floor through all three dead nights.

So whatever changed, it is not the water acting as a nightly barrier — it wasn't there to act. Two readings survive:

  • It learned something once and stopped coming. The spray drove it off the patio on 09-07/08, and it has essentially not returned. Under this reading the fixture is doing the work — the cat avoids the place, or the visible cannon, without needing a fresh demonstration. The 05:20 visit fits: it came, stopped, looked at the water cannon, and left with nothing firing.
  • Something unrelated to us changed. A cat's range, a rival, a new food source, weather. Seven nights is not long, and we have no control patio to compare against.

Three reasons to hold this loosely. The siren-opener change went live on 09-06, one night before the water — the two are confounded and cannot be separated from this data. Two of the seven "after" nights were partially blind, so those zeros are a floor rather than a count; Dima's 05:20 sighting is the proof, since it happened during exactly such a gap. And seven nights against seven is a small sample for a nocturnal animal whose visit counts already swung between 2 and 4 before anything changed.

What would firm it up is simply more nights — the streak either continues or it doesn't. What would settle the mechanism is the cat returning and being watched: if it stops short again with nothing firing, the fixture-avoidance reading gains sharply.

Questions

  1. Does the streak reach ten nights? That is roughly where two-of-four-per-night dropping to zero stops being explicable as ordinary variation.
  2. If it returns, does it stop short? The one observation that would separate "it learned to avoid the place" from "it happened to be elsewhere".
  3. Do the real 0.4-second pulses deliver water? Still unanswered from the on-site test, and still the most important unknown about the hardware — though, on this fortnight's evidence, possibly not the most important unknown about the problem.

The Blind Hours

Five and three-quarter hours of clean watching, then the link to Dima's house went away and took the cameras and the deterrent with it — for the rest of the night, and still counting this morning.

Night of 2026-09-10 → 11 · window 21:45–06:00 · covered 5h 47m · blind 2h 28m

This is not a third no-show. It is half a night. Nothing orange was seen between 21:45 and 03:31:55, and after that we saw nothing at all — no cameras, no Home Assistant, no water, no sound.

The food was out and the deterrent was dead for the last two and a half hours of the window. And the stray came — at 05:20, straight through the gap. Dima watched it on his own NVR: it stopped at the right-hand end of the patio, looked at the water cannon, and left without going in. See 05:20 below; it is the most interesting thing to happen in a week.

The order matters. The RTSP port refused connections before the host became unreachable — services stopping first, then the network stack. That is what a device shutting down or rebooting looks like, not a link flapping.

Our side is not the fault

Worth stating precisely, because it is the part we can act on and it checks out clean:

  • Our internet was up throughout (1.1.1.1 reachable).
  • wg-quick@wg0 has been continuously active since Sep 7 15:58 — three days without a restart, including through the whole outage.
  • The peer is a dynamic-DNS name, pdx.oops.wtf:51820, and a wg-reresolve timer re-resolves it every 30 seconds — confirmed firing. So a changed IP cannot explain a three-hour outage; we would have picked it up within half a minute.
  • That name still resolves, to 184.3.245.239. The address answers neither ICMP nor TCP/443 — not conclusive on its own, since UniFi gear routinely drops both.

None of that proves the fault is at Dima's, and I will not claim it does. But every link on our side of the tunnel is accounted for, and the failure signature is a device going away. The question for Dima is simply whether anything at his house lost power or rebooted around 03:31:55.

Three instruments, three green lights

This is the third distinct failure in four nights where a liveness check reported health while the thing it stood for was broken. They are worth listing together, because the pattern is now the finding:

ping 192.168.1.133
Missed the HA service stop on 09-09/10 entirely — the host answers ping perfectly well while Home Assistant is dead.
GET /api/
Returned 200 API running at 00:40:02 while the valve, the failsafe and both speakers were still unregistered. Overstated readiness by ~90 seconds.
systemctl is-active
wg-quick@wg0
Reports active right now, three hours into a dead tunnel. It describes a unit that started successfully in September, not a link carrying packets. The overnight report prints this as "tunnel: active".

Each one measures a process rather than a product — the same lesson this project keeps relearning, in a third costume. The only probes that have not lied are the ones that ask for the actual artefact: a segment's age, a frame's timestamp, the valve entity's state, a ping to the far end.

05:20 — the visit we did not see

Dima's account, in his words: "at 5:20 the OC came. He shows up at the very right end of the frame in the middle, stops. No sound is played at him, water is not shot. He looked at the water cannon and went away."

Placed against our logs: 05:20 is 1 hour 48 minutes after we went dark and 40 minutes before the window closed. The cat approached from the right-hand end of the frame — which, if it is the right-hand lane rather than the gate, is the wetter of the two approach routes, the one where 56% of the path crosses the spray zone. It stopped there. Nothing fired, because nothing could.

What this establishes

The stray came, stopped short, and left without entering and without any deterrent firing at all. The food was out. It had a free run at the flap and did not take it.

What it does not establish

That the cannon caused it. "Looked at the water cannon" is an interpretation of where a cat's head was pointing — the behaviour is solid, the attribution is inference.

Two readings fit, and they point in opposite directions:

  • The cannon deters on sight. The cat has learned that the object predicts the unpleasant thing, and now avoids without needing a demonstration. If so the deterrent is working better than designed — it no longer has to fire — and the two preceding no-show nights look like the same effect rather than coincidence.
  • The cat simply isn't very motivated at the moment, and would have turned back regardless. In which case some of what we have been crediting to sound and water was never ours to claim.

This is the closest thing to a control trial the project has had, and that cuts both ways. Every previous success took the form deterrent fires → cat leaves, and we read the arrow as causal. Last night ran the other condition by accident — deterrent absent → cat came, stopped, left anyway — and produced the same outcome. One trial cannot separate "the hardware's presence is now sufficient" from "the cat would have left either way", but it does mean the causal story is no longer the only one on the table, and the honest position is that our recent wins are less securely attributed than they looked yesterday.

What would separate them: whether the cat, on a night when the system is live, still stops short at the same distance. If it does, the cannon-on-sight reading gains. If it walks in whenever nothing fires, the motivation reading gains — and the deterrent has to prove itself again.
